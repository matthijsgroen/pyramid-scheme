# What the player knows about Egypt, and where the game says it

A beat that depends on something the player has never been told is not a payoff. It is a private joke.

The audience is children and adults with no Egyptology, in a dozen countries. **Every belief a beat leans
on has to be taught inside the game**, and the game is already good at this — `fez.json`'s mosaic panels
do it five times without ever sounding like a lesson.

## The rule

**Teach it at least two acts before the beat that needs it, in passing, in a character's mouth.**

- _Two acts earlier_, because teaching it at the moment of use telegraphs the payoff. A player told what
  cats were for and then immediately shown a cat has been handed the answer and the question together.
- _In passing_, because a belief stated in order to be used later reads as a lesson. `mosaicStarter` gets
  away with it by being about a cat in a doorway first and a goddess third.
- _In a character's mouth_, because Fez being wrong about it later is what proves the player was listening.

## What the story assumes, and whether it is covered

| Assumption                                     | Beat that needs it                          | Taught                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **A name surviving IS surviving**              | **the ending**                              | **nowhere — the gap**                  |
| Tombs were stocked with goods for an afterlife | Fez's whole trade, the forgery, the economy | assumed, never said                    |
| Hieroglyphs are writing, and learnable         | reading, the Sphinx                         | `mosaicJunior` (Thoth) + every tableau |
| The heart is weighed against a feather         | the mural's finale                          | `mosaicWizard`                         |
| Dead is not finished                           | old sites reopening; the backwards map      | `mosaicMaster` (Osiris)                |
| Cats guarded the grain                         | Act I's running joke                        | `mosaicStarter`, and Ipi corrects Fez  |
| Tombs were robbed, long ago                    | Act III, and the forgery                    | Act III's arrival line                 |
| Lesser burials held imitations                 | Act II → Act IV's twist                     | Act II, `junior_2`                     |

Six of eight are covered, one is assumed, and **the one the ending stands on is not taught at all**.

## The gap: why a name matters

Egyptians wrote a name in a cartouche, carved it where it would last, and erased an enemy's to end them.
Saying the name of the dead was how the dead continued. Without that, the finale is _"we read a name. So
what?"_ — a payoff built on knowledge the player was never given.

**It costs about two lines, and Ipi is the right mouth.** He is a bookkeeper who wants his accounts
settled, three thousand years on, and the belief is indistinguishable from his character:

> **Explorer:** Why does it matter, if it is all written down?
>
> **Ipi:** Because a thing written down is a thing that happened. Rub out a man's name and he never did.

Act I, from the first ghost, two acts before Henut's wall and four before the Sphinx. Henut then reinforces
it as feeling rather than fact — _"It is the only thing that does."_ — and by Act V the player has been told
twice, by two people who cared, without either of them explaining an ending.

## The other gap, smaller

**Nobody says what the tombs were for.** The player loots them for fifty hours without being told the goods
were provisions for a journey, which is what makes Fez's trade a joke and the burial economy a scheme. One
line, and Act I's merchant route is the place.

## When a new arc is written

Add its assumptions to the table above, and answer two questions before authoring a single beat:

1. **Does a beat fail without it?** If yes it is load-bearing and needs teaching. If no, leave it as
   texture and let the curious player enjoy noticing.
2. **Who says it, and how many acts earlier?** If the answer is "the narrator, just before", the beat is
   not ready.
