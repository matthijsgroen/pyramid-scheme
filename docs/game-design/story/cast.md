# The cast

Who these people are, kept in one place so copy written months apart still sounds like them. An arc's own
Cast table (`FORMAT.md` §2) lists who appears in it; this says who they are.

**The most load-bearing section of each sheet is _Never says_.** Voice notes drift, because everyone reads
"dry" differently. A list of things a character does not do survives being read by someone else, and it is
the only part of this document that can be checked.

What is deliberately absent: biography, family, home towns, childhood. For the explorer that is not merely
unnecessary — every detail of a past implies a demographic this game has decided not to have.

---

## Fez

**Wants.** Things worth selling. He is not on a pilgrimage and says so. By the end he wants one thing not
to be for sale, which is the whole of his arc.

**Voice.** Excitable, commercial, warm. Speaks in _we_. Short sentences, frequent exclamations, opinions
about value arriving unasked. He teaches by enthusiasm rather than instruction — he points at a thing and
tells you why it mattered to the people who made it.

Shipped lines that are the reference:

> **Fez:** Wow, our first treasure! Well done!
>
> **Fez:** A cat in the doorway, and a snake leaving in a hurry. That was worth a bowl of milk.
>
> **Fez:** Everything on this counter I carried out of somewhere. That one came from two tombs back.

**He is right about trade and people, shaky about gods and kings.** A merchant knows what things cost, who
owed whom, and which stories get told in markets. He is not a scholar, and letting him be confidently
slightly wrong about a god is what keeps the game from being a lecture — and gives someone else something
to correct.

**Never says.**

- Never explains a rule the board should be teaching. That is the boards' job, and it is how he became a
  tooltip the first time.
- Never threatens, and is never the source of menace.
- Never moralises about money at the player, however much the ending is about value.
- Never refers to the explorer by name or pronoun.
- Never claims scholarship he has not got.

**Arc.** Prices everything → uneasy about what he has been pricing → will not name a price at all.

## The explorer

**Not the player.** There is no customisation and none is wanted: the job is a character anybody is glad to
be beside, which is a different and easier job than a blank to project onto.

**Wants.** At the start, something ordinary — a find worth the trip, the X on a map they arrived holding.
Then they meet writing they cannot read, and by the end that is what they came for. **The swap is the
story**, which is why the stated goal at the start has to be small.

**Voice.** Dry, brief, present tense. Asks the question the player is already asking. Answers in
fragments. Competent — never the ignorant one, just not a scholar either.

> **Explorer:** Gone, or never there?
>
> **Explorer:** It was a door.
>
> **Explorer:** You like that he's owed money.
>
> **Explorer:** Noted.

**Never says.**

- **No gendered self-description.** French, Italian, Polish and the rest gender adjectives and past
  participles, so _"I was surprised"_ forces a translator to pick. Present tense, questions, and
  observations about the world.
- No name, no pronoun for themselves beyond a present-tense "I".
- No backstory: no family in the trade, no schooling, no home.
- Never exclaims. Enthusiasm is Fez's register and the joke dies if both of them have it.
- Never cruel. Dry is not mean, and they never deflate Fez in a way that wounds him.
- Never explains a rule — they are working it out too.

**Arc.** Came for a find → leaves able to read the thing they could not read on the first morning.

## The pair

Two good sheets do not add up to a double act, so this is the one that matters.

**What each brings.** The explorer has the map and the compass — both are in the art. Fez has the country,
the gossip, and somewhere to sell things. **Neither of them is the expert**, which the opening already says
out loud: _"Can you read it?" / "No. Can you?" / "No."_ Two people out of their depth is a better premise
than a mentor and a novice, and it keeps the explorer from being the ignorant one merely for being new.

**The engine.** Fez invites himself. He is not hired and not asked; he attaches, and by the end refers to
it as a partnership. The explorer does not agree so much as fail to object — which is their register, and
avoids making them accept something the player did not choose.

**The comedy.** Fez enthuses, the explorer deflates, and nobody is humiliated. Fez gets the volume, the
explorer gets the last word, and the last word is short.

**Never.** They never say they are friends, and neither of them ever says the relationship out loud. It is
shown once, at the end, by what he will not sell.

### The relationship across the five acts

| Act | Fez                        | Shown through                                                 |
| --- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I   | transactional, and says so | states his reason while it is still true                      |
| II  | notices how they work      | the first beat that is about the explorer, not about the loot |
| III | defers                     | asks what they think before saying what he thinks             |
| IV  | worried                    | wants to leave, stays anyway                                  |
| V   | will not sell it           | the decided line — _"That's his record of us."_               |

That line already says **us**. The middle three acts are what earns the word.

## How they meet — open

Ruled out: **they already know each other.** The arc above needs distance to travel, and "I came for the
resale, I'm saying it now while it's still true" is a strange thing to say to an old partner.

| Setup                                              | Exposition              | Consequences                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fez narrates from line one** (today)             | all front-loaded        | no meeting, no runway for the arc, and the copy that does it predates the explorer entirely                                                                                                                                     |
| **They meet at the Sphinx**                        | one scene, very early   | meeting and hook land together; the first board still gets explained at the player                                                                                                                                              |
| **Explorer alone; Fez found in the first pyramid** | split across two places | **the opening board has no companion, so it has to teach itself** — the wordless first instance, forced by the fiction rather than queued behind nine mechanics. Costs one board of quiet, and a dry character alone is silence |
| **Fez arrives later still** (the first shop)       | very split              | a long stretch with no companion in a game whose companion is the entertainment                                                                                                                                                 |

**The third is the strongest** and the argument is not a story one: it makes the review's onboarding
complaint structurally impossible rather than scheduled. It also fits the other review item — the exterior
board should be the ceremony of opening a site once and never again, which is the shape of a thing that
teaches itself.

If it is taken, the meeting wants to be **a voice before a face**: the player solves a wall, goes in, and
something in the dark says they are doing it wrong. What is a lizard in a fez doing down here is a better
first question than who are you — and he is complaining rather than looming, which is what keeps it comic.

### Open

1. **Which setup**, and if the third: how long is the quiet, exactly one board or the whole first pyramid?
2. **What is on the explorer's map at the start?** It wants to be small and ordinary, and it must not be
   the inscription — that is the thing that replaces it.
3. **Does Fez get one thing he is wrong about per tier**, or is being shaky about gods an occasional
   colour? A running one is funnier and needs someone to correct him.
